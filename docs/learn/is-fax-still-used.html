Is fax still used? The numbers, checked at the source

Last checked 2026-10-19

Fax is still in daily use in German offices, Japanese schools and US doctors' offices. The figures you usually see quoted for it are often wrong. Here are the ones that check out, each with its source and year.

The short answer

Yes. Not everywhere, and less every year, but in some places a lot more than you would guess. It is less about old machines and more about who is on the other end. A government office, a payer or a hospital records department publishes a fax number, and the people who need something from them send faxes. Most of those faxes now leave a PC and arrive as a PDF. It is still fax.

Below are the numbers we could check at the source, country by country, then a list of figures that get repeated a lot and do not hold up. Every number has its source and year next to it. The full list is at the bottom.

Germany: three offices in four

Bitkom, the German digital industry association, has asked the same question since 2018. In early 2024 it phoned 604 companies with 20 or more employees. 77% still used fax (Bitkom, 2024), down from 82% in 2023, 88% in 2022, 92% in 2020 and 95% in 2018. One in four (25%) faxed often or very often, down from 49% in 2018 (Bitkom, 2024).

It is declining, slowly. Bitkom's own words: the change is happening erstaunlich langsam, surprisingly slowly. The reasons are worth reading, because they are not nostalgia (Bitkom, 2024, companies that fax):

  • 56%: unavoidable with the authorities.
  • 43%: safer than the post.
  • 35%: the fax process works and is established.
  • 27%: habit. Another 27%: they need a delivery confirmation.

And 72% of German companies that fax send at least some of it as fax-from-email, the "modern standard"; 24% use nothing else (Bitkom, 2024). The paper is going away faster than the fax is.

Japan: the schools and the ministries

Japan's education ministry (MEXT) set a goal of ending hanko seals and fax in schools, "in principle", by fiscal 2025. Its own self-check of 28,049 public elementary and junior high schools, published in March 2026, found 71.7% still using fax in daily work and 91.0% with documents that still need a seal (MEXT, 2026). The first round, in December 2023, had fax at 95.9% of schools. Most of it went to private suppliers (70%), boards of education (58%) and school-lunch centres (32%) (MEXT, 2023).

That is real progress, from 95.9% to 71.7% in about two years, and it is still most schools. The central government tried the same thing in 2021. After a June notice told ministries to stop faxing, the reform office received about 400 responses from officials arguing to keep it, many on security grounds (The Japan Times, 2021).

The UK: the NHS and its fax ban

In July 2018 the Royal College of Surgeons used Freedom of Information requests to count 8,209 fax machines in NHS hospital trusts in England. Newcastle upon Tyne had the most, 603 (RCS, 2018). Five months later the Health and Social Care Secretary banned the NHS from buying new fax machines from January 2019 and set 31 March 2020 as the date for removing them (DHSC, 2018). By September 2019, FOI replies suggested about 42% had gone (Healthcare IT News, 2019).

The NHS is the one large system here that tried to end fax by order, and it is the clearest example of how long that takes even with a deadline.

The US: healthcare, mostly

In a November 2019 MGMA poll, 89% of US medical practices said they used a fax machine (n=1,581). Their top uses were sharing records, referrals, labs and results, payers, and pharmacies (MGMA, 2019). The volume is widely reported as about 9 billion (2019) fax pages a year in US healthcare. We could not find the original measurement behind that figure, so read it as an estimate.

Prior authorization, where an office asks an insurer for approval, is the one place it is measured carefully. The 2024 CAQH Index found US medical prior authorizations were 35% fully electronic, 43% through payer portals and 22% fully manual. Fully manual means phone, mail, fax or email; CAQH does not split fax out. Fully manual was 33% in 2022 (CAQH, 2024). Each manual request took providers 24 minutes against 16 through a portal (CAQH, 2024). The same report notes a federal rule requiring insurers to offer electronic prior-authorization connections by January 2027 (CAQH, 2024, citing CMS).

So the trend is the same as Germany and Japan. Fax is shrinking where someone builds a working alternative on both ends, and staying where they don't.

The market: growing, by how much depends on whose report

You will see "the fax market is growing 13% a year" a lot. Here are two reports from the same research firm, side by side:

Same firm, similar subject, and the starting sizes are about seven times apart. Most of that is definition: what counts as "online" against "cloud", and whether consumer services are in. Both point the same way, up, by roughly 11-13% a year. Neither size should be quoted on its own as "the fax market".

For a hard number, the biggest listed fax company is a better guide than the analysts. Consensus Cloud Solutions, which owns eFax, reported about 638,000 small-office online fax customers and about 65,000 corporate customers in its annual report for 2025, roughly 703,000 in all (Consensus 10-K, 2025).

Where email wins (and where it doesn't)

We make a fax app, so here is the part a fax company usually leaves out. For most messages, email is better.

JobWhich is better, and whyWinner
Cost per messageEmail. Free to send; fax costs a line and, on most services, pages.Email
Colour, photos, big filesEmail. Fax is black and white at fax resolution; a colour photo arrives as a grey smudge.Email
Back-and-forthEmail. Reply, thread, forward, cc. Fax has no thread.Email
Finding it laterEmail, out of the box. A fax is a picture until someone runs OCR on it.Email
On your phoneEmail. Most fax setups were built for a desk.Email
Reaching a stranger's officeFax. A fax number is printed on the form, the letterhead and the payer's website; nobody needs to give you an address or an account.Fax
Proof it arrivedFax, usually. The receiving machine answers and the sender gets a transmission result. 27% of German faxers say they keep faxing because they need that confirmation (Bitkom, 2024).Fax
The other side requires itFax, because you have no choice. 56% of German companies that fax say they cannot avoid it with authorities (Bitkom, 2024).Fax
SecurityNeither, by default. Ordinary email can be read on any server it passes through; a fax can land face-up on a shared machine. Each can be done carefully. Neither is safe just because of what it is.Tie

Look at the right-hand column. Fax wins where the other office decides how you talk to them. Email wins everywhere else. That matches the reasons German companies gave Bitkom: authorities, delivery confirmation, and processes that already work.

Questions

Is fax still used in 2026?
Yes, in specific places. 77% of German companies with 20 or more staff still used fax in 2024 (Bitkom, 2024); 71.7% of Japanese public elementary and junior high schools used it in daily work in the latest ministry self-check (MEXT, 2026); and 89% of US medical practices told MGMA in 2019 that they had a fax machine.
Which industries still rely on fax?
Healthcare is the biggest: referrals, records, lab results, pharmacy and payer communication (MGMA, 2019). Government is the other driver - in Germany, communication with authorities is the most common reason companies keep faxing (56%, Bitkom 2024).
How many fax pages does US healthcare send a year?
It is widely reported as about 9 billion (2019). We could not find the original measurement, so treat it as an estimate.
Is the fax market growing?
Analysts say yes, but their sizes disagree. Verified Market Research puts online fax at $4.18 billion in 2024, growing 13.06% a year, and cloud fax at $559.4 million in 2025, growing 11.5% a year. The direction agrees; the size does not.
Is fax more secure than email?
Not automatically. Fax gives a delivery result and does not sit in a mailbox, but it can print on a shared machine. Ordinary email can be read on the servers it passes through. How you handle each one matters more than which you pick.
